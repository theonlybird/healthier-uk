03/06/2026 • By Dr William Bird MBE

Health creation is not a softer word for prevention

The two terms get used interchangeably in policy documents. They describe genuinely different work, and conflating them costs us.

Prevention is done to a population: screening, vaccination, risk stratification. It is essential, and the NHS is reasonably good at it. Health creation is done with a community — it starts from what people already have rather than what they lack.

The practical difference shows up in who holds the budget. A prevention programme is commissioned. A health creation programme is grown, and usually by someone who is not paid to do it.

The question to ask of any local scheme is simple: if the funding stopped tomorrow, would anything survive? If the answer is no, it was a project, not health creation.

Across the coalition we keep meeting the same pattern — a handful of people with local credibility, a modest amount of money, and permission to act. That combination outperforms far larger programmes with striking regularity.
