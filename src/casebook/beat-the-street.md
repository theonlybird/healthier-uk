healthChallenge: |-
  Like many communities, Reading faces challenges around physical inactivity, health inequalities and opportunities for residents to build regular movement into daily routines. Local partners wanted an approach that could reach beyond people already engaged in sport and exercise and create a visible, community-wide reason to move more. The programme also aimed to encourage active travel, strengthen connections with local places and provide a simple way for schools, families and residents to participate together.
organisations: "Intelligent Health; local schools; public health teams; community organisations"
actions: |-
  Beat the Street transformed Reading into a real-world activity game for 6 weeks. Participants were given a card or used the Beat the Street app and earned points by walking, cycling or wheeling between specially installed Beat Boxes positioned around the local area.
  
  Schools, community organisations and local partners helped promote the programme and encourage participation. Players joined teams, followed their progress on leaderboards and took part in additional challenges designed to encourage people to explore different parts of their community.
  
  The game was designed to make physical activity feel accessible and enjoyable rather than presenting it simply as exercise. By placing Beat Boxes near schools, local centres, parks, walking routes and other frequently visited locations, participants were encouraged to incorporate additional movement into journeys they were already making.
  
  Communications throughout the game celebrated participation, highlighted local places to visit and encouraged residents to continue building active habits. Activity data and participant feedback were also collected to understand how people engaged with the programme and its effect on physical activity.
outcomes: |-

  Evaluation showed that the programme successfully reached a broad cross-section of the community, including people who were previously inactive. Participants reported walking, cycling or wheeling more frequently and described Beat the Street as providing additional motivation to get outside, explore their local area and be active with other people.
  
  Schools were an important route into the wider community, with children encouraging parents, carers and other household members to participate. The shared game also helped create conversations about physical activity beyond traditional sport settings.
  
  Where follow-up evaluation is available, results can be added here to demonstrate changes in physical activity before and after the programme, particularly among less active participants.
  
  Beyond the headline activity figures, local feedback highlighted the value of providing a simple, free and inclusive activity that gave people a reason to make small changes to their everyday routines.
lessons: |-
  One of the strongest lessons from Beat the Street is that physical activity interventions can engage a much wider audience when movement is built into a shared experience rather than presented solely as exercise.
  
  Schools provide an effective starting point for engagement, but strong community participation also depends on reaching adults, workplaces, community organisations and residents who do not have a connection to a participating school.
  
  The positioning of Beat Boxes is also important. Locations need to create attractive, walkable routes and connect places that residents already use, while also encouraging people to explore their wider neighbourhood.
  
  Clear communication throughout the programme helps maintain momentum. Regular challenges, local stories and achievable goals can encourage people to continue participating after the initial excitement of launch.
  
  Finally, the programme works best when local partners view Beat the Street as more than a short-term game. Linking participation with wider physical activity, active travel and community initiatives creates opportunities to build on the engagement generated during the programme and support longer-term behaviour change.
